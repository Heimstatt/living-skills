## Job

Help a team reconstruct existing market boundaries and value-cost assumptions so it can formulate
a differentiated, lower-cost value hypothesis and expose the conditions required for adoption.

## Core method

1. Make the current strategic logic and competing factors visible.
2. Search beyond accepted industry boundaries, existing customers, and product conventions.
3. Examine utility across the buyer experience and barriers preventing use or adoption.
4. Decide which factors to eliminate, reduce, raise, and create.
5. Describe the proposed value profile and its cost logic.
6. test conceptual coherence through buyer utility, accessible price, target cost, and adoption.

## Decision rules

- A proposed factor belongs in the reconstruction only if it changes buyer value, cost, or both.
- Noncustomers are a search direction, not automatically a target segment.
- Differentiation without a credible cost logic is incomplete; cost reduction without buyer value
  is ordinary efficiency work.
- Failure at an earlier strategic-sequence stage should be resolved before polishing later stages.
- Every elegant canvas claim remains a hypothesis until external evidence supports it.

## Expected result

A Market Creation Hypothesis that explains how and why the value profile changes, which cost
assumptions change, what blocks adoption, and what evidence would falsify the logic.

## Anti-patterns and exclusions

- Renaming features into eliminate/reduce/raise/create boxes without changing market logic.
- Declaring competition irrelevant because a diagram looks divergent.
- Inventing noncustomer motives or prices.
- Applying the method before the current market or search space is defined.
- Treating a strategic hypothesis as validated product-market fit.
